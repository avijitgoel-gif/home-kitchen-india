## Plate warmer or steam box: the one choice that matters

Almost every electric lunch box under ₹1,500 is a **plate warmer**. A small heating element sits in the base, the steel containers stack on top, and a thermostat stops the food from scorching. These draw very little power. Cello says the Atom works on 15 watts, NEXX says the Hott-2 uses 90 watts and Jaypee Plus lists 100 watts for the Hottline 4. Low power means slow heating: Milton quotes 15 minutes for the two container Swiftron and 30 minutes for the four container Electron, and AGARO quotes 25 to 35 minutes.

The newer type is the **steam box**. You pour a little water into the base, lock the containers in and switch on. The Xech Hot Box II is rated at 300W and Xech says it reheats in about 2 minutes and can cook simple food such as eggs, oats or idli in about 20 minutes. It also lists auto cut-off and dry-boil protection.

## How long can food sit before you plug it in?

This is the question nobody selling these boxes answers. The US Department of Agriculture says bacteria grow fast between 40°F and 140°F (about 4°C to 60°C) and that perishable food should not sit at room temperature for more than 2 hours, or 1 hour when it is above 90°F (about 32°C). It also says reheated leftovers should reach 165°F (about 74°C).

An electric lunch box packed at 8 am and plugged in at 1 pm has spent five hours in the danger zone, which is longer than that guidance allows. Low watt warmers are designed to make food pleasantly hot, not to bring it to a boil. So pack food you cooked that morning, keep the box in an office fridge if there is one, and do not treat a plate warmer as a way to make old food safe.

## Is ISI mark compulsory for electric lunch boxes?

Not clearly. The Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026 (S.O. 1739(E)) makes the ISI mark under IS 302 (Part 1):2024 compulsory for 90 appliance types from 1 October 2026, with small enterprises getting until 1 January 2027 and micro enterprises until 1 April 2027. Electric lunch boxes are not named. The nearest entries are "warming plates and similar appliances" and, for steam boxes, "electric steam cookers". None of the listings we checked shows an ISI licence for the lunch box itself (Xech mentions an ISI-marked cord). Look for a CM/L number on the box when it arrives, and prefer a brand with a stated warranty and Indian service address.

## How to choose an electric lunch box

### 1. Count your containers

Two small containers (500 to 520 ml) suit a light meal or a snack. A full rice, dal and sabzi lunch needs four containers, around 1.3 to 1.7 litres, like the Milton Electron or Jaypee Plus Hottline 4.

### 2. Check what the containers are made of

Every pick here has steel containers. 304 stainless steel is stated by Xech and NEXX. The outer body is plastic on almost all of them, so never put the base in water.

### 3. Leak seals matter more than heating

Dal leaking into a laptop bag is the worry, and Amazon's review summaries rate leak-proofing as mixed for Milton, Jaypee Plus and Cello. Look for a silicone gasket (AGARO, Jaypee Plus Hotpot) or locking lids, and carry the box upright.

### 4. Warranty

Milton, Jaypee Plus Hottline 4, AGARO, Cello and NEXX state 1 year. Xech, BONJOUR and the Jaypee Hotpot do not on their listings.
