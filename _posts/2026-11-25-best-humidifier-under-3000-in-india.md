## Do you need a humidifier? The 30 to 50 percent rule

Mayo Clinic says it is ideal to keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%. Below that, dry air can irritate your nose, throat and eyes and dry out your skin. Above it, water can condense on walls and windows, and damp surfaces encourage mould, bacteria and dust mites, which can trigger allergies and asthma.

So buy a digital hygrometer first; simple ones cost a few hundred rupees on Amazon.in (one was shown on these product pages at ₹295). If your bedroom already reads 50% or more, as it often does in coastal cities like Mumbai or Chennai, a humidifier will only make the room damp. If it reads in the 20s or low 30s with the heater on, a humidifier helps.

### White dust and the water you use

All six humidifiers here are ultrasonic: a vibrating plate turns water into a fine cool mist. Whatever is dissolved in the water goes into the air too. Mayo Clinic notes that tap water minerals often show up as white dust on furniture, and that you might breathe some of them in. Its advice is to use distilled or demineralized water, and demineralization cartridges if the maker suggests them.

In Indian homes, the practical version is RO purified water rather than hard borewell or tank water. The raydrop is the only listing here that names a demineralization cartridge.

### Cleaning is not optional

Mayo Clinic's routine for cool mist and ultrasonic models: empty, dry and refill the tank every day if you can, and clean the humidifier every three days with 3% hydrogen peroxide (sold at chemists), then rinse. Do not add anything else to the water. AGARO's listing suggests weekly cleaning and Dr Trust's says monthly. Follow Mayo's more frequent schedule.

### What about essential oils?

Three of these listings (AGARO, raydrop and Dr Trust) advertise aroma or essential oil use. Mayo Clinic says plainly that humidifiers only add moisture and cannot be used to breathe in products such as essential oils, and COSTAR's own listing warns against adding oils or fragrances to the tank. If you want a scent, buy a separate diffuser and keep the humidifier water plain.

## How to choose a humidifier

### Tank size by room and routine

For a bedroom used at night, 2 to 2.5 litres is enough and keeps the unit small. For a living room, or if you want to fill it once a day, look at 4 to 6 litres.

### Top fill and placement

Top fill tanks (raydrop, COSTAR) save effort when you refill daily. Keep the unit on a flat surface away from curtains and electronics; Mayo Clinic says to turn it down if windows, carpets or curtains get damp.
