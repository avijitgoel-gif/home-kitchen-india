## Why sweaters pill in the first place

Woolmark, the wool industry body, explains that pilling is caused by rubbing during wear. It shows up most around the elbows, armpits, sleeves, belly and sides, where the arms keep brushing against the body of the sweater. Short fibres work loose from the yarn, tangle into a ball and stay anchored to the fabric by a few fibres. Loosely spun, soft yarns pill more than tightly twisted ones.

That anchoring is why picking pills off by hand takes forever and why a sticky lint roller does not help much. A roller lifts loose hair and dust from the surface, but a pill is still attached to the garment. A lint remover works like a tiny rotary shaver: the pills poke through holes in a metal mesh and spinning blades behind the mesh cut them off into a small container.

One honest caveat: every shave removes some surface fibre. One Amazon buyer of the AGARO LR2007 notes that it can take away some of the softness of the cloth. Use it when the pills bother you, not every week.

## Buying guide: what to check before you buy

### Corded, rechargeable or AA batteries

Corded models (AGARO Elite, both Novas) never fade mid job and have no battery to age, but you need a plug point and the cord can feel short. Rechargeable models (AGARO LR2007, Portronics, Lifelong) are easier on a sofa or bed. AA powered shavers like the Philips need fresh cells, and weak cells mean slower blades.

### Check the brand field, not just the title

Search for "Nova lint remover" on Amazon.in and you will see several listings using the Nova name. One is sold by Nova Retail, one shows SHOPTOSHOP as the brand, and another we checked (about ₹279, 4.0 from 10,376 ratings) mentions Nova in its bullet points but lists ALLISABLE as the brand. Scroll to "Product information" and check the brand, importer and warranty before you buy, so you know whom to call if it fails.

### Do not trust pooled ratings blindly

When two models share one rating count, like the AGARO LR2007 and Elite, the number reflects the whole family. Click through to the reviews and filter by the exact variant if you can.

### Blades and mesh

More blades and a mesh with several hole sizes help on thick, heavily pilled fabric. A fine mesh is safer on delicate knits. Philips advises smoothing the garment on a flat surface, not pressing too hard and keeping away from embroidery, zips, buttons and loose threads. That advice applies to every model here.

### Safety and cleaning

Always switch off (and unplug, or remove the batteries) before opening the head to clean. Philips' manual says to keep fingers away from the blades during cleaning and never open the blade guard while it runs. AGARO's interlock, which stops the motor if the mesh is loose, is a useful extra. Empty the lint container after every garment or two, or the suction drops.

### Does a lint remover damage clothes?

It shaves off pills, which are made of the garment's own fibres, so each use removes a little surface fibre. Used gently on a flat surface with the mesh fitted, it does not cut the fabric. Avoid embroidery, zips, buttons and loose threads, and do not press hard.

Corded models keep full speed and have no battery to wear out, which suits big batches of blankets. Rechargeable ones are more convenient for quick touch ups on a sofa or a sweater on your lap. Both types are available under ₹1,000.
