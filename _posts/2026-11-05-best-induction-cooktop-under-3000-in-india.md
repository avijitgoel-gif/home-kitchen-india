## How to choose an induction cooktop

### 1. Check your cookware first

Induction heats the pan itself through a magnetic field, so it only works with cookware that has iron in its base. The simple test recommended by Consumer Reports and GE Appliances: if a fridge magnet sticks firmly to the bottom of the pan, it will work. Cast iron, carbon steel and magnetic stainless steel work. Plain aluminium, copper and glass usually do not, unless they have a magnetic base layer. Many Indian brands now print "induction base" on the box.

### 2. 1600W is enough for most homes

1600W to 1800W handles tea, dal, rice and sabzi for a small family. 2000W boils faster and suits larger families or pressure cooking every day.

### 3. Know the running cost

A cooktop running at its full 2000W for one hour uses 2 kWh, or 2 units of electricity. In practice it cycles below full power for most dishes, so real use is lower. Multiply the units by your state's tariff to estimate cost.

### 4. Look for surge protection if your power is unstable

Prestige lists 4KV surge protection on all three of its models here. That is useful in areas with frequent voltage fluctuation.

### 5. Ignore the MRP

The MRPs in the table run from about 1.4 to 2.2 times the selling price. Compare selling prices and rating counts instead.
