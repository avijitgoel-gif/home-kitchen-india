## Why the litre number misleads

Every cooker here is sold as 1 litre or 1.8 litre, which is the pot. What you care about is how much raw rice goes in, and the listings handle that very differently:

- Prestige states it plainly: 0.4 litre raw for its 1 litre cooker.
- Panasonic says 600 g raw for the 1 litre SR-WA10, and both 1 kg and 0.6 kg on different parts of the same SR-WA18 page.
- V-Guard says "up to 6 cups" for its 1.8 litre model.
- Pigeon gives a number of people instead.

When a listing gives no raw figure, use the people count and review comments on size, and assume less than you hope. For one or two people, 1 litre is enough. For three or four, 1.8 litre.

## Auto switch off or keep warm?

This is the second thing to check, and listings rarely put it in the title.

- **Keep warm** cookers (Pigeon Joy 1.8 L, V-Guard VRC, Prestige Delight) move to a warm setting when the rice is done. Pigeon says up to 5 hours.
- **Auto switch off** cookers (the Panasonic WA models) stop heating once cooking ends. Fine if you serve at once.

A Panasonic rice cooker manual attached to the SR-WA18 listing adds two habits that help with any cooker: wipe the outside of the pan dry before putting it in, because a wet pan can make noise and damage the unit, and leave the lid closed for at least 15 minutes after the switch pops up so the rice can steam. It also tells you to keep the base of the pan and the heating plate clean, since anything between them causes the cooker to malfunction. Bits of rice on the plate are a common cause of half cooked rice.

## The BIS mark to look for: an R-number, not ISI

Rice cookers are not certified the same way as most kitchen appliances. They sit under the Electronics and Information Technology Goods (Requirement for Compulsory Registration) Order, 2012, run by MeitY and BIS. BIS lists "Rice Cooker" to IS 302-2-15:2009 on its compulsory registration product list with an effective date of 1 April 2021, and BIS granted the first rice cooker registration in September 2020. IS 302 (Part 2/Sec 15) is the safety standard for appliances for heating liquids.

In practice this means the box or rating plate should show the BIS Standard Mark with a registration number that starts with "R-", followed by eight digits. BIS's own example is R-41164917, the first rice cooker licence. It is a safety registration, not a quality rating. Be wary of unbranded cookers with no R-number at all.

## What a pot of rice costs in electricity

Very little. A 700W cooker that heats for 30 minutes uses 700 x 30 / 60 = 350 watt hours, or 0.35 units. At ₹8 per unit that is about ₹2.80. A 400W cooker running the same 30 minutes uses 0.2 units, about ₹1.60. Twice a day for a month, the 700W cooker comes to about 21 units, or roughly ₹168. Keep warm uses less power than cooking but adds up over hours, so unplug when you are done.
