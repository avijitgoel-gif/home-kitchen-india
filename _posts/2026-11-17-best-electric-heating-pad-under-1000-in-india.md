## Heating belt or gel hot water bag: which type do you need?

| | Plug-in heating belt | Rechargeable gel hot water bag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it heats | Wire element, warm as long as it is plugged in | Gel heated for a few minutes, then unplugged |
| Heat control | Usually 3 settings on a controller | One temperature, set by its thermostat |
| How long it stays warm | As long as you run it (most makers say 15 to 20 minute sessions) | Claims range from 45 minutes (RARECARE) to 2 to 4 hours (Caresmith) |

If you want to strap heat to your back while you work, buy a belt. If you want a cordless hot water bottle you can carry to bed for a while and do not want to boil water, buy a gel bag. Never use either while asleep (more on safety below).

## Is the ISI mark compulsory for heating pads?

Yes, from this season. The Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026 (S.O. 1739(E)) lists "Blankets, Pads, Clothing and Similar Flexible Heating Appliances" at serial number 43. It requires the BIS Standard Mark (ISI) for appliances up to 250V single phase from 1 October 2026, with later dates of 1 January 2027 for small enterprises and 1 April 2027 for micro enterprises. BIS also established IS 302 (Part 2/Sec 17):2026, based on IEC 60335-2-17:2022, as the specific safety standard for these products on 6 July 2026.

None of the seven listings above mentions an ISI mark or BIS licence number. Older stock and small sellers may still be legal under the later deadlines, but it is worth checking the box or the product for the ISI mark and a CM/L licence number, which you can verify on the BIS website.

## How to use a heating pad safely

Heating pad burns usually happen at low temperatures over a long time, not from one hot touch. The warnings in heating pad manuals and US FDA adverse event reports repeat the same rules:

- **Never fall asleep with it on.** Burns can happen on any setting. Tata 1mg, HealthSense and the US maker Conair all say this in their instructions.
- **Keep sessions short.** Tata 1mg says 15 to 20 minutes at a time.
- **Start on low** and check the skin under the pad often.
- **Put the pad on top of the body part, not under it.** Do not lie or sit on a belt, and avoid sharp folds.
- **Always use the cover,** or a towel between the pad and your skin.
- **Do not use it on numb skin**, or on someone who cannot feel heat well or move it away: young children, people with diabetes-related nerve damage or poor circulation, or anyone unconscious. Check with a doctor first.
- **Never use it with heat rubs** or pain balms. Conair warns these can cause skin burns.
- **Throw it out** if the cord, controller or inner cover is cracked, scorched or blistered.
- **For gel bags:** charge only on a flat hard surface, unplug before use, and never use one while it is charging.

For period pain, the UK's NHS suggests a heat pad or hot water bottle wrapped in a tea towel on your tummy, along with warm baths, gentle exercise and painkillers. It says to see a doctor if periods become more painful or the pain stops your daily activities.

Very little. The Tata 1mg belt is rated at 65W. A 20 minute session uses 65 x 20 / 60 = about 21.7 watt hours, or 0.022 units. At ₹8 per unit that is under 20 paise. ## How to choose an electric heating pad

### 1. Size and strap

For the lower back, a belt of around 35 x 25 cm, like the MEDTECH HP11 (37 x 27 cm), covers the area properly. For the knee or shoulder, the strap matters more than the size.

### 2. Auto shut-off and thermostat

Look for both a thermostat (it holds the temperature) and an auto shut-off (it turns the pad off after a set time). Several listings say "auto shut-off" without saying how long, so read the manual when it arrives.

### 3. Washable cover

A removable, washable cover is far more hygienic. The HealthSense cover is spot clean only, while the Flamingo XL has a washable outer cover.

### 4. Warranty

Only HealthSense, Flamingo and RARECARE clearly state 1 year on their listings. For a product that runs hot against your skin, that matters.
