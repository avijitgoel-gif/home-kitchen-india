## Does "1g precision" mean the scale is accurate to 1 g?

No. "1g precision" on a listing tells you the display moves in 1 g steps. It says nothing about how close that reading is to the true weight. A scale can show 1,000 g for a load that is really 990 g. None of the listings we checked publish an accuracy tolerance, so the only way to know is to test your own scale.

Also look at the smallest weight it reads. The HealthSense KS 25 starts at 5 g and the Themisto at 3 g. Put less than that on the platform and the display may stay at zero.

## The free coin test: check your scale at home

Indian coins are made to a fixed weight, which makes them handy test weights. According to SPMCIL, the government mint, the current series ₹1 coin weighs 3.09 g and the ₹5 coin weighs 6.74 g. The ₹10 coin from the same 2019 series weighs 7.74 g.

1. Put the scale on a flat, hard counter, switch it on and let it settle at zero.
2. Place ten ₹1 coins from the 2019 or later series (the small 20 mm ones). The reading should be close to 31 g.
3. Try ten ₹5 coins from the same series. You should see about 67 g.
4. For a bigger load, weigh 1 litre of water in a container you have already tared. Water weighs about 1 g per ml, so you should see about 1,000 g.

Coins wear over the years, so allow a gram or two. If your new scale is off by much more, exchange it inside the return window.

Do not use a sealed 1 kg packet of atta or sugar as your test weight. Under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, a packet labelled 1 kg is allowed to be up to 15 g short, so it is not a reliable reference.

## Can you use a home kitchen scale in a shop?

No. Under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, any weight or measure used in a transaction must be verified and stamped by the legal metrology department. The Delhi Weights and Measures Department says stamping for electronic scales is valid for one year at a time. A ₹259 home scale from Amazon is meant for your kitchen, not for selling sweets, vegetables or homemade food by weight. If you run a shop, buy a stamped trade scale from a licensed dealer. ## How to choose a kitchen weighing scale

### 1. Capacity

A 5 kg scale is enough for baking and portion weighing. Pick 10 kg if you weigh atta for the month, a whole watermelon, or heavy steel vessels. Remember that the bowl counts towards the limit even after you tare it.

### 2. Platform

A stainless steel top (ATOM, HealthSense KS 25, Tata 1mg) is easier to wipe and does not stain with haldi. Plastic tops are lighter but can scratch.

### 3. Units

Grams are all most Indian recipes need. Ounces and pounds help with American baking recipes. An ml mode for water or milk is handy but only approximate for anything other than water.

### 4. Battery and auto off

Most scales here use AAA cells or a CR2032 coin cell. Auto off saves battery but can switch off in the middle of a recipe, so tare again if the screen goes blank.

### 5. Warranty

Warranties run from 6 months (ATOM) to 1 year (HealthSense, RARECARE). At under ₹300 a short warranty is fine. At ₹600 to ₹700 you should expect a full year.

### Are cheap kitchen scales accurate?

Many are good enough for cooking, but listings only state the display step (1 g), not a tested accuracy. Check yours with ten ₹1 coins of the 2019 series, which should read about 31 g, or a litre of water, which should read about 1,000 g.

### Should I buy a 5 kg or 10 kg kitchen scale?

5 kg is enough for baking and portion control. Choose 10 kg if you weigh large quantities of atta or rice, or heavy vessels, since the container counts towards the limit.

### Can I use a kitchen scale to sell things?

No. Scales used for buying or selling must be verified and stamped under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009. Home kitchen scales are for personal use only.

### What is the tare button for?

Tare sets the display back to zero with a bowl on the scale, so you see only the weight of what you add. You can tare again between ingredients and weigh everything in one bowl.
