## Corded or rechargeable: decide this first

**Corded choppers** (AGARO 400W, Pigeon Ultra Fast, Inalsa Bullet, Glen, Sujata) run on 230V mains with motors rated from 250W to 400W. You press the top and the blades spin until you let go.

**Rechargeable choppers** (AGARO Elite, Pigeon Zoom, UN1QUE) charge over USB and run on a small battery. Pigeon lists the Zoom at 30W and 5V DC with a 1,500 mAh battery, and says a 4 hour charge gives about 30 minutes of use, or 100+ chops of 10 to 15 seconds each. UN1QUE also lists 30W. That is roughly a tenth of the power of a corded model, so they are best for garlic, ginger, chillies and a single small onion.

## Buying guide: what to check before you buy

### The bowl size in the title is not what you can chop

Several listings print the biggest number they can. Inalsa gives 0.9 litre total but only 0.5 litre processing capacity for the Bullet. Glen's title says 500 ml while each of its two bowls is listed at 400 ml. Compare processing capacity where the brand gives it.

### The time rating: run it in bursts

Small chopper motors are not built to run for long stretches. Inalsa's Bullet listing states a time rating of "1 min continuous (max.)" in its specifications, while the same page's marketing line says it can work continuously for 3 minutes. Go by the lower figure. Sujata tells owners to operate the Chopcraft in short cycles, and Pigeon says each chop on the Zoom takes 10 to 15 seconds. In practice, pulse for a few seconds at a time, and let the motor rest if it smells hot.

### Wattage tells you what it can handle

A 300W to 400W corded chopper will deal with carrots, nuts and bread for crumbs. A 30W rechargeable chopper is for soft and small items. If you want wet chutneys or masala pastes, a mixer grinder is a better tool than any chopper.

### Interlock and blades

AGARO and Pigeon say the motor only runs when the top is locked onto the bowl, Sujata lists a micro switch safety lock and UN1QUE stops the blades when the bowl is opened. The blades are very sharp, so lift them out by the plastic hub.

### Where it is made and who services it

Half the models here are imported from China, which is normal in this category. All eight listings name an Indian importer or manufacturer with a customer care contact. A household electrical appliances Quality Control Order (S.O. 1739(E)) also made the ISI mark mandatory for most mains powered household appliances from 1 October 2026, with later dates for small and micro enterprises, so for corded models it is worth looking for ISI on the box.

## FAQ

### Which is the best electric chopper under ₹2,000 in India?

The AGARO 400W Electric Chopper at about ₹1,549. It has a 400W motor, a 500 ml bowl, a 4.2 rating from over 4,000 ratings and is made in India. For under ₹1,000, the Pigeon Ultra Fast 300W at about ₹809 is the best value.

### Is a rechargeable chopper as good as a corded one?

No. Rechargeable models such as the Pigeon Zoom and UN1QUE are listed at 30W against 300W to 400W for corded ones, and their bowls hold 250 ml to 350 ml. They are fine for garlic, ginger and chillies but slow for a family's onions.

### How long can I run an electric chopper continuously?

Use short pulses. The INALSA Bullet's specifications list a time rating of 1 minute continuous at most, and Sujata advises short cycles. Most chopping takes 10 to 15 seconds.

### Can an electric chopper make chutney or ginger garlic paste?

It can make a coarse paste with a little oil or water, but it is built for chopping and mincing. For smooth chutneys and masala pastes, a mixer grinder works better.

### Can I wash the chopper bowl in a dishwasher?

It depends on the model. Inalsa and UN1QUE list their bowls and blades as dishwasher safe, while AGARO and Pigeon say hand wash. Never put the motor unit in water.
