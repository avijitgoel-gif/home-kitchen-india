## How to choose a hand blender

### 1. Match wattage to your use

For lassi, shakes, eggs and thin soups, 180W to 300W is usually enough. For thick purees, nut-based mixes or daily use, look at 400W and above. A higher number is not automatically better, but it gives you more headroom.

### 2. Look at the stem material

Steel stems resist staining and odours from turmeric and tomato, which plastic stems pick up. Check the product title or description for "stainless steel".

### 3. Know the running cost

A 400W blender running for 5 minutes uses roughly 0.03 units of electricity (400W x 5/60 hours / 1000). Even a 1000W model used for 5 minutes uses about 0.08 units. Running cost is not a deciding factor at this price.

### 4. Check accessories and warranty

Some listings include a whisk, chopper or beaker. Others sell only the stem and motor unit. Read the "what is in the box" section before buying.

### 5. Ignore the MRP

The MRPs here run up to about 1.8 times the selling price. Compare selling prices and rating counts instead.

### Is a hand blender better than a mixer grinder?
They do different jobs. A hand blender is better for soups, lassi and pureeing in the pot. A mixer grinder is better for wet and dry grinding, such as chutney and masala. See our [mixer grinder guide]({{ "/best-mixer-grinder-under-3500-in-india/" | relative_url }}).

### How many watts do I need in a hand blender?
180W to 300W handles light jobs such as shakes and lassi. Choose 400W or more for thicker mixes or daily use.
