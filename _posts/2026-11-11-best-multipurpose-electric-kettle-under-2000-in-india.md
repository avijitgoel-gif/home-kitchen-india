A multipurpose electric kettle, sometimes sold as a multi kettle or multicooker, is a small steel pot on a 360 degree power base with a low wattage element and a temperature knob. Unlike a normal kettle that boils water and switches off, it can simmer, so you can make Maggi, oats, soup, tea, boiled eggs and, with a steamer tray, idli or momos. That is why it is the default cooking gadget in hostels, PGs and offices, and a handy backup for any home when the gas cylinder runs out.

## How to choose a multipurpose kettle

### 1. Understand what 600W means

Most multi kettles use a 600W element, less than half the power of a 1,500W water kettle. That keeps food from burning instantly, but it is slow. AGARO buyers mention about 10 minutes to boil water. The 800W KENT is a bit quicker.

### 2. These pots simmer, they do not fry

The thermostat switches the element on and off to hold a temperature. Several buyers of the Amazon Basics and Pigeon models say it keeps cutting out when they try to cook with little water. Use these for anything cooked in water or steam. Do not expect to fry, roast or make thick curries.

### 3. Be careful with milk

Milk is the most common complaint across all five listings. Thin steel bottoms and concentrated elements scorch milk and leave stains. If you must heat milk, use the lowest setting, stir often and never leave it.

### 4. Check the steel grade

Only some listings state the grade. The Amazon Basics says SS201. When a listing does not say 304, assume it is a cheaper grade and dry the pot after each use to avoid rust spots.

### 5. Look for the ISI mark

From 1 October 2026, the Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026 requires covered appliances sold in India to carry the BIS ISI mark under IS 302 (Part 1):2024. Its list includes appliances for heating liquids, the category compliance consultants map kettles to. Small manufacturers get until 1 January 2027 and micro enterprises until 1 April 2027, and declared older stock can be sold for up to six months, so you will still see unmarked stock for a while. Check for the ISI mark on the box or base when it arrives.
